CSD-615

Chinese Stroke Dysarthria 615 — a Mandarin dysarthric speech corpus with independent severity ratings from three senior clinicians.

Overview

CSD-615 contains 615 Mandarin speech samples collected from stroke patients at tertiary hospitals in China. Each participant performed a simple counting task — counting from one to ten in Mandarin through a dedicated mobile application.

Every sample was independently rated by three chief physicians with more than 10 years of stroke experience, on a four-level severity scale: 0 Normal, 1 Mild, 2 Moderate, 3 Severe. The median of the three ratings is provided as the reference label.

Unlike existing dysarthria corpora, CSD-615 releases the individual clinician ratings alongside the audio rather than only a consensus label, making it suitable for multi-annotator learning and for studying inter-rater variability in perceptual dysarthria assessment.

Where the disagreement lives

Clinicians agree almost perfectly on clear cases and diverge sharply on borderline ones. Unanimous agreement is 91.9 % for Normal and 79.7 % for Severe, but collapses to 27.9 % for Mild and 32.5 % for Moderate — even though all three raters share the same training and protocol. Disagreements are overwhelmingly one level apart, concentrating on the Normal/Mild and Mild/Moderate boundaries.

This is the property that motivates releasing the individual ratings rather than a single consensus label.

Access

This page hosts only the eight preview clips above, plus manifest_anonymized.csv — the per-sample severity ratings (sample_id, site, split, rater_a, rater_b, rater_c, median) for all 615 samples, with both sample and site identifiers replaced by codes.

The full 615-sample corpus, including the three individual clinician ratings per sample, is available for non-commercial research use upon request.

To request access, open an issue in the repository or contact the authors with your name and institutional affiliation, a short description of the intended research use, and agreement to the terms of use below. Requests are reviewed individually and access may be granted subject to the terms of the original ethics approval.

Ethics

All data were collected under institutional ethics approval with written informed consent from each participant, who consented to the use of their recordings for research purposes.

Audio previews on this page contain no personally identifying metadata: no participant identifiers, contact details, or hospital names are published. The clips are labelled with a within-repo index only.
